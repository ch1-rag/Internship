V1 — Savings Pool Mobilisation
Odisha's ₹6,44,120 Cr in bank deposits (FY26) are 8.8× MF AAUM. Every FD that matures is a natural conversion moment — the depositor has liquidity, is making an active financial decision, and is already in contact with a bank or distributor. This vector asks: what if a small fraction of that pool were redirected into MFs at maturity?
Basis for the formula
Why this structure? Why these numbers?
Why total deposits, not just term deposits? Term deposits (₹3,79,718 Cr) are the primary substitution target, but savings account holders also present a conversion opportunity for liquid MF products (liquid/money-market funds). Using total deposits gives a conservative, inclusive base.
Why 3%–5%? Peer-state evidence: high-penetration states (Maharashtra, Gujarat) have seen FD-to-MF conversion averaging 1–3% of deposit base per year over the last decade. Odisha's conservative scenario models the low end of this. 5% over 5 years = 1% per year — achievable without structural change.
Why not higher? FD holders are behaviorally conservative (Odisha term deposit/GSDP of 37.5% vs national median 30.2%). A 10%+ shift would require a major rate differential event — not modelled here.

V2 — Investor Base Expansion
Odisha has 28.52 lakh registered MF investors — just 6.05% of its 4.71 crore population. The EPFO pipeline alone adds 1,73,160 new formal workers (18–35 age group) every year who are prime SIP candidates. This vector sizes the AUM impact of closing the gap between Odisha's 6.05% penetration and 8%, 10%, or 12% targets.
Basis for the formula
Why this structure? Why these numbers?
Why total population as the base, not working-age? AMFI's own investor penetration metric uses total population (registered investors ÷ total population). Using the same denominator keeps comparisons consistent with national benchmarks (national avg ~9.05%).
Why 8%–12% target range? 8% = minimum defensible target: just approaching national average with organic pipeline. 10% = national average today. 12% = peer benchmark of states with similar income levels but better distribution (e.g. Andhra Pradesh at 11.6%).
Why use current AUM per investor (₹2,57,573)? Conservative assumption — new investors enter at today's average. In reality, new retail investors start small (first SIP). Actual near-term AUM is 40–60% of these figures; these represent the 5–7 year steady-state once investors have had time to accumulate.

V3 — Female Investor Inclusion
Women are 52.5% of Odisha's population but only 21.3% of its MF investors. Female investor penetration is just 2.59% vs 9.47% for males. The key finding: female investors hold ₹3,55,630 AUM each — nearly 2× the male average of ₹1,82,649. The gap is distribution access, not financial depth. V3 sizes the AUM from reaching 5–6% female penetration.
Basis for the formula
Why this structure? Why these numbers?
Why use female population as the denominator (not total)? Female penetration is measured on the female population base to track the gender-specific gap accurately. Using total population would dilute the metric.
Why target 5%–6% (not higher)? National female investor penetration is ~4–5% of female population. Odisha at 2.59% is roughly half the national female benchmark. 5% = reaching national female norm. 6% = modest stretch above national female norm. Both are achievable within 5 years with targeted SHG-SIP and EPFO onboarding programmes.
Why use ₹3,55,630 (female rate) instead of the overall ₹2,57,573? Female investors who are already invested in Odisha tend to be urban-adjacent, higher-income — hence their higher AUM. As the programme reaches rural/semi-urban women, the rate will converge toward the overall average. Using the current female rate is a current-cohort assumption; the dsbox below flags this explicitly.

V4 — Per-Capita AUM Deepening
This vector does not add new investors. It measures how much AUM grows when existing investors invest more — through rising incomes, step-up SIPs, longer tenures, and market appreciation. Odisha's AAUM per capita is ₹15,581 vs Jharkhand's ₹20,367 — the target is to close that gap over 5 years.
Basis for the formula
Why this structure? Why these numbers?
Why total AAUM per capita — not SIP AUM per capita? SIP AUM per capita (₹4,933) captures only the SIP portion. Total AAUM per capita (₹15,581) captures all deepening effects: SIP appreciation, lump-sum investments, step-up SIP amounts, and market returns. The vector is measuring mutual fund wealth per person, not just SIP activity.
Why Jharkhand as the ambitious target? Jharkhand is structurally near-identical to Odisha: mining-heavy economy, no large metro centre, similar per-capita GSDP (~₹12,600 vs ₹12,400), similar urbanisation rate. Yet Jharkhand's AAUM/GSDP is 16.2% vs Odisha's 7.4% — more than double. If Jharkhand can achieve ₹20,367/capita, Odisha can too. It is an achievable baseline, not an aspirational ceiling.
Why +₹3,000/capita as the conservative floor? At Odisha's current AUM/capita CAGR of ~7.6%/year, +₹3,000 over 5 years is roughly what happens organically even without new programmes. It is the no-intervention baseline.

V5 — Distribution Expansion
Odisha has 3,620 registered ARN holders (MFD licence holders). At ₹20.3 Cr AUM per MFD vs ₹40.0 Cr nationally, Odisha's MFDs are under-productive — not because demand is weak, but because they are geographically concentrated. New MFDs in unserved districts would bring new investors and raise per-MFD productivity. Conservative target: match Jharkhand's ARN density (+1,549 MFDs). Ambitious: reach national ARN sub-total density (+2,960 MFDs).
Basis for the formula
Why this structure? Why these numbers?
Why ARN holder count (3,620) — not grand total (7,157)? ARN holders are the decision-making units who can be targeted for recruitment. Employees (grand total includes 3,537 employees) are not independently recruitable as new MFDs. The gap to Jharkhand is therefore computed on the ARN-holder base, which is more actionable.
Why current AUM/MFD (₹20.3 Cr) as the productivity assumption? New MFDs in unserved geographies are assumed to ramp up to the current Odisha average over 5 years. In year 1, a new MFD might achieve ₹5–8 Cr. Using ₹20.3 Cr is a 5-year steady-state assumption — which is why V5 is also a 5–7 year horizon figure.
Why Jharkhand as conservative and national sub-total as ambitious? Jharkhand (+1,549) is achievable with focused AMFI MFD recruitment in 3 priority zones. Reaching national ARN density (+2,960) requires full deployment across all 30 districts — ambitious but not unrealistic given Odisha's EPFO and NPS pipeline.

Start here: what is "overlap" and why does it matter?
The pizza analogy Imagine you run a pizza shop and you have five plans to grow revenue: (1) sell to new neighbourhoods, (2) add female customers, (3) upsell existing customers to larger pizzas, (4) open new delivery zones, (5) run a discount campaign. If you add up the revenue from all five plans separately, you'll overcount — because the same customer (say, a woman in a new neighbourhood who orders a large pizza through a new delivery zone after seeing your discount) gets counted in four plans at once. The overlap adjustment removes that double-counting. Each customer's pizza revenue is real — you just shouldn't count her four times.
The five vectors work the same way. A new female investor (V3) also: counts as a new investor in V2, may have converted from an FD (V1), and was likely reached by a new MFD (V5). Her ₹3,55,630 of AUM is real — but if you add V1+V2+V3+V4+V5 directly, you have counted her money up to four times. The overlap adjustment removes this double-counting from the raw sum to arrive at the true incremental AUM.
The four types of overlap in this model
Type 1 — V3 ⊂ V2: "The subset problem" (always 100%) Every new female investor counted in V3 is also counted in V2 as a new investor. This is a mathematical certainty — V3 is literally a subset of V2. There is no scenario where a woman joins as an MF investor and is not counted in the investor base expansion. This overlap is irreducible and fixed at 100% of V3's AUM — regardless of programme design or scenario.
Type 2 — V5 → V2: "The enabler problem" (partially overlapping) New MFDs (V5) onboard new investors — those are the same people counted in V2's penetration target. If +1,549 MFDs each onboard ~400 investors, that's ~6.2 lakh new investors, which is already inside V2's +9.34 lakh target. About 70% of V5's AUM at conservative level is AUM that V2 also claims.
Type 3 — V1 ∩ V2: "The FD converter problem" (partially overlapping) When an FD holder converts to MF (V1), some fraction of them were previously unregistered — they are new investors. V1 counts the AUM from their converted deposit; V2 also counts the AUM they bring as a new investor. About 30% of V1 converters at conservative level are simultaneously new investors in V2's count.
Type 4 — V4 ∩ V2: "The accumulation problem" (partial, growing over time) V4 measures rising AUM per capita. Some of that rise happens because new investors (counted in V2) accumulate over time. At conservative level, ~10% of V4's deepening is attributable to V2's new investors growing their AUM. At ambitious level, this rises to ~20% as more of V4's target comes from newer investors.
Why 0% and 100% overlap are both impossible
0% overlap is impossible Even if every other pair of vectors were perfectly independent, Type 1 (V3 ⊂ V2) alone means overlap is at minimum ~15.4% of the raw sum (₹16,189 Cr ÷ ₹1,05,160 Cr). You cannot eliminate this — it is a logical necessity, not a programme design choice.
100% overlap is impossible V4 deepens AUM for existing investors (people already in the system). V1 converts FD holders who have never invested in MFs. These are structurally different people. Their AUM cannot be the same rupees. Running all five vectors simultaneously always produces more total AUM than any single vector — so some additionality always exists.
Step-by-step: how the 20%–55% range is derived
1
Start with the raw sum (conservative, t=0) Add up all five vectors at their conservative values. This is the naive total — what you'd get if there were zero overlap.
V1=₹19,324 + V2=₹24,058 + V3=₹16,189 + V4=₹14,144 + V5=₹31,445 Raw sum = ₹1,05,160 Cr
2
Remove Type 1 overlap (V3 ⊂ V2) — the certain part V3 conservative AUM = ₹16,189 Cr. This entire amount is already inside V2's estimate. Remove it from the double-count.
Overlap from V3⊂V2 = ₹16,189 Cr ÷ ₹1,05,160 Cr = 15.4% of raw sum
Running overlap estimate: 15.4%
3
Add Type 2 overlap (V5 → V2) — the enabler part V5 conservative = +1,549 MFDs. Each MFD onboards ~400 investors at ₹2,57,573 AUM each.
V5-driven new investors = 1,549 × 400 = 6,19,600 people Their AUM (already in V2) = 6,196 × ₹2,57,573 ÷ 100 = ₹15,960 Cr As % of raw sum = ₹15,960 ÷ ₹1,05,160 = +15.2pp
Running overlap estimate: 15.4 + 15.2 = 30.6%
4
Add Type 3 overlap (V1 ∩ V2) — the FD converter part ~30% of the people who convert their FDs to MFs (V1) were previously unregistered investors. V2 also counts them.
V1-V2 shared AUM = ₹19,324 Cr × 30% = ₹5,797 Cr As % of raw sum = ₹5,797 ÷ ₹1,05,160 = +5.5pp
Running overlap estimate: 30.6 + 5.5 = 36.1%
5
Correct for triple-counting (V2 ∩ V3 ∩ V5) Some female investors (V3) who were onboarded by new MFDs (V5) got subtracted twice — once in Step 2 and once in Step 3. Add back the triple-counted portion.
~40% of V3's new female investors also came through new MFDs (V5) Triple-count correction = ₹16,189 × 40% × 40% = ₹2,590 Cr As % of raw sum = −2.5pp (add back, reduces overlap)
Running overlap estimate: 36.1 − 2.5 = 33.6%
6
Add an uncertainty buffer for unmeasured interactions There are 3-way and 4-way interactions that are real but too complex to precisely quantify (e.g. V1∩V2∩V5: an FD converter who is female and was reached by a new MFD). We add a conservative buffer.
Uncertainty buffer = +5 to +10pp at conservative level Conservative midpoint = 33.6 + 7 = 40.6% → rounded to 42% midpoint Simulator uses 45% (slightly above midpoint — appropriately cautious)
Conservative overlap: ~42–48%, simulator uses 45%
7
At ambitious (t=100), overlap falls — why? At ambitious scale, programmes are better-coordinated and reach more distinct populations. V5 ambitious MFDs penetrate deep rural areas (populations not yet in V2's urban-skewed pool). V1 ambitious intercepts FD holders who are genuinely first-time investors (less V1∩V2 overlap). Result: overlap falls from ~45% to ~25% as the vectors become more complementary and less redundant.
Ambitious overlap range: ~26–32% → simulator uses 25% Moderate overlap: midpoint = 35%
Conservative (45% overlap): ₹1,05,160 Cr × 0.55 = ₹57,838 Cr incremental → Projected AAUM ₹1,31,298 Cr
Moderate (35% overlap): ~₹1,50,000 Cr raw × 0.65 = ~₹97,500 Cr incremental (varies by slider)
Ambitious (25% overlap): ₹2,10,192 Cr × 0.75 = ₹1,57,644 Cr incremental → Projected AAUM ₹2,31,104 Cr
Summary: floor, ceiling, and pairwise matrix
Floor: 20%
V3⊂V2 alone = 15.4% of conservative raw sum. Adding the minimum V5→V2 residual even under perfect programme coordination (~5%) gives 20.4%. Rounded to 20% as the hard floor. No programme design can eliminate these structural overlaps.
Ceiling: 55%
Conservative pairwise estimate (42–48%) + 7pp uncertainty buffer = 49–55%. At 55%, incremental AUM = ₹1,05,160 × 0.45 = ₹47,322 Cr — the most pessimistic planning scenario, appropriate for year-1 budget estimates with no coordination yet established.

This simulator was built as a planning and communication tool — not a financial model. It translates structural data (deposit base, EPFO pipeline, MFD density, demographic gaps) into a quantified range of outcomes under different programme assumptions. The value is not the single output number — it is the ability to ask "what if" questions, stress-test assumptions, and align multiple stakeholders on a shared understanding of where the market opportunity lies and how big each lever actually is.

MFDs — Mutual Fund Distributors
Understanding the size of the local market and making the case for expansion
An MFD in Bhubaneswar or Cuttack may not think of themselves as part of a ₹73,460 Cr market. This tool makes the scale of the opportunity visible — and quantifies what each additional client, each FD conversion, each SIP step-up is worth in aggregate.
  • Understand that V1 (savings pool mobilisation) is the single largest near-term opportunity — every FD maturity conversation with a client is a contribution to ₹19–32K Cr of potential incremental AUM
  • Use the V4 per-capita deepening frame to prioritise annual SIP step-up conversations with existing clients over new client acquisition
  • See that Odisha's female investor opportunity (V3) represents ₹16–23K Cr — and that female clients hold nearly 2× the AUM of male clients on average
  • Make the case to a reluctant prospect: "Odisha is at 7.4% AAUM/GSDP — Jharkhand with the same economy is at 16.2%. You are in an early market."

What this dashboard is — and is not
✓ What it is
  • A structured scenario planning tool grounded in secondary data
  • A communication device that translates structural gaps into rupee terms
  • A prioritisation framework: which lever produces the most AUM per unit of effort?
  • A monitoring template: track actual AAUM growth vs conservative/moderate/ambitious benchmarks annually
  • An adaptable template for other under-penetrated states
✗ What it is not
  • A financial projection or revenue forecast (figures are opportunity sizing, not guaranteed outcomes)
  • A year-1 AUM target (all figures are 5–7 year steady-state estimates)
  • A substitute for district-level market research or field validation
  • A precise model — the overlap adjustment involves judgment; the slider is intentionally transparent about this
  • Applicable to metro-heavy states where institutional/HNI flows dominate AUM (this model is calibrated for retail-SIP dominated markets)
